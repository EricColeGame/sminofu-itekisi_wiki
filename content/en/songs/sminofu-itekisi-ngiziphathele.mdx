## The Sound: Maskandi Roots With a Modern Finish

Maskandi is a genre built on interlocking guitar lines, call-and-response vocals, and lyrics that function as social commentary as much as entertainment. *iTekisi* leans into that tradition without treating it as a museum piece. The production, handled with EMPIRE's backing, keeps the guitars forward in the mix while giving the low end and percussion enough room to translate on phone speakers — which, for most South African listeners, is where an album actually gets judged.

A few patterns emerge across the 17 tracks:

- **Storytelling first.** Songs like "Umuntu Ongenacala" and "Imizamo" build around narrative verses rather than a single repeated hook, which rewards close listening.
- **Pacing variety.** The album alternates between brisk, danceable numbers and slower, reflective passages, so an hour-long listen does not flatten out.
- **Language and code-switching.** Titles such as "Why Umehlulela" show Sminofu mixing isiZulu phrasing with English, a common and increasingly visible habit in contemporary Maskandi.
- **Place as a theme.** "Madagascar," "Es'thenjini," and "eMachobeni" all gesture toward locations, a classic Maskandi device for grounding a song in a real setting.
